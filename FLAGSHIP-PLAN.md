# Slate flagship plan: operational memory and sustained inquiry

**Requirements and current scope.** Slate v0.2.0 defaults to the core-wiki profile. Federation, granted actions and sustained inquiry remain explicit flagship development functionality with full acceptance pending. [ACCEPTANCE.md](./ACCEPTANCE.md) governs all 220 current product clauses, 22 baseline obligations, eight policy profiles and six journeys. The requirements below remain binding; they do not establish provider, prospective-value or public-release acceptance. [HANDOVER.md](./HANDOVER.md) records current source behavior and verification limits.

## The outcome Slate owns

Slate should take a team from a real question or piece of work to a sound answer, decision, completed action, and maintained knowledge. It is a collaborative workspace for shared operational memory and sustained inquiry: the team can investigate questions whose answers do not yet exist, test what it learns, and make the resulting method cheaper to repeat.

People begin where work already happens: an existing Slate page, an assigned task, a support case in HappyToHelp, or a plain-language request. Slate gathers permitted context, finds the useful source revisions, prepares and checks the analysis, carries out work covered by existing authority, and keeps the result current. It coordinates parallel investigations and follows through across waits. Members should not create claim tables, graphs, schemas, skill definitions, research plans, model choices, or agent handoffs to get a result. Slate prepares that structure and keeps it inspectable when useful.

Ask for an outcome, not a new workspace setup project. The first useful result should come from the pages and connections already available. If a material source or effect genuinely needs new access, Slate asks the named owner for the smallest meaningful grant, recommends a default, and explains the consequence of granting or declining. It counts provider setup, admin work, and colleague contributions as work, rather than promising setup-free access Slate does not have.

## One workspace, many useful ways in

Slate remains a page-first shared workspace. The editor, tree, comments, sharing, revision history, imports, structured records, and explicit publishing stay first-class for people who need to author, review, organize, discuss, or teach together. The product extends this accepted collaboration model; it does not replace it with chat.

A task, result, decision, or research question is a view over the same workspace records, not a new module users must learn or maintain. Open the relevant view from the page, assigned work, or change that brought it into focus. Evidence, source revisions, impact, cost, agent activity, and controls are available when someone asks why or needs to intervene. The main result stays readable and makes material uncertainty, paid effects, audience, and unfinished work clear. Today shows meaningful outcomes and decisions that truly need a person; routine sync health, every run, and background maintenance do not become a progress-monitoring job.

## What a useful day feels like

A specialist opens an SSO question already being handled in HappyToHelp. Slate checks approved terms, release status, account state, and the current article in parallel. It gives the specialist a concise, source-linked answer: whether the customer is eligible, the recorded price and interval, what setup remains and who performs it, and whether the account is actually enabled. Asking a question never buys or activates the add-on. If the evidence is incomplete, Slate says what is unknown. It repairs an article independently when an exact publication grant covers it; otherwise it leaves a ready-to-review patch. HappyToHelp retains the customer conversation and reply.

When the answer requires investigation, Slate continues beyond retrieval. For “Why is time to customer value rising, and what could reduce it without adding support work?”, Slate checks the measurement, tests rival explanations, prepares a bounded experiment, and reports an early useful finding. A named owner is asked only to choose a genuine business objective or authorize an effect the existing instructions do not cover. Slate records the actual result, distinguishes benefit from null, adverse, or inconclusive evidence, and updates the reusable method. The research and its calculations are prepared by Slate; the person can inspect or change them without being made the research coordinator.

Before a policy decision is accepted, Slate prepares a read-only impact rehearsal: affected accounts and exceptions, dependent answers, articles, procedures, skills, and open work. The accountable people discuss the evidence and accept a new immutable decision in its page. Slate then applies determinate repairs within standing grants, checks them, and reports the outcomes together. An exact delegated publication grant can authorize a named external update subject to source-audience and container permissions; Slate asks only when authority or a real policy choice is missing. It does not queue every procedure for ritual approval when a repair is already delegated.

A new colleague can start from an assigned task and receive the current procedure, the reason it applies, and the owning system's completion check. Slate assembles and tests that procedure from accepted decisions and real cases. The colleague supplies only facts no connected source can establish or performs work requiring their action. Discovering an outdated instruction creates a focused repair item linked to its source revision; it does not require the colleague to learn Slate's internals.

## Authority and careful completion

Existing authorized instructions and standing grants are reused without repeated approval. Slate compiles them into narrow internal capabilities for each run and checks source access and effect scope again before acting. A broad goal does not grant permission to disclose information to a new audience, publish a page, spend money, purchase an add-on, or accept a policy decision. Publication requires explicit authority for the exact page or named class of updates, plus container permission; a valid standing grant avoids repeated approval within that scope. A provider credential or source permission that has not been granted still needs its named owner.

If a true decision is missing, Slate asks one concise question with a recommended default, alternatives, uncertainty, and consequences. Routine maintenance completes quietly within its grant; meaningful pending, failed, or unconfirmed states stay visible. A request receipt is not an enabled account. A possible external write is not success until reconciled. Human **Verified** status remains an attestation by a person who opened that exact revision; machine freshness checks never renew it.

Accepted decisions remain immutable and source citations stay pinned to exact revisions. A Slack message can be evidence without being policy authority; an article cannot establish a live account state; model confidence cannot settle an ownership question. When access changes, dependent answers and outputs are rechecked before reuse. Exact sources, scopes, authority, cost, impact, and receipts remain available without making every user read a data model.

## Why this is worth building

The flagship requirements extend the core wiki into a complete evidence → authority → action → repair loop across systems, inside the same collaborative workspace, with owner-controlled data and checkable outcomes. [CONTRACT.md](./CONTRACT.md) specifies the required behavior; the [frontier extension](./FRONTIER-EXTENSION.md) describes sustained investigation, bounded experiments and the evidence needed to establish value.

The measure is total work and cost to a sound, maintained outcome: every attempt, model and tool cost, elapsed time, connection and deployment work, expert interruption, owner review, colleague setup, failure, and later repair. Compare the manual incumbent workflow, an uncustomized Slate path, an owner-customized Slate path, and a capable generic agent with the same permitted sources and tools. Better models strengthen the premise that hard work can be delegated; they do not remove the need to measure quality, authority, and full lifecycle cost. Acceptance keeps the complete three minimum-release workflows, the full flagship, the sustained-inquiry loop, and all 220 current product clauses and 22 baseline obligations.
