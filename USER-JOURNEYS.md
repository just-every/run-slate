# Slate flagship user journeys

**Accepted requirements; qualification pending.** The owner accepted the full flagship and sustained-inquiry scope on 27 September 2026. [ACCEPTANCE.md](./ACCEPTANCE.md) governs all 220 current product clauses, 22 baseline obligations, all eight policy profiles and all six journeys. The historical 0/191 foundation count does not describe the current source. Examples below specify required behavior, not evidence of provider, prospective-value or public-release acceptance. Current execution and remaining gates are recorded in [HANDOVER.md](./HANDOVER.md).

The interaction rule is simple: people name the outcome; Slate gathers, checks, and follows through under existing authority. Results show uncertainty, paid effects, audience, and pending work. “Why?” opens sources, revisions, scope, authority, cost, impact, and receipts. Pages, comments, tree, sharing, history, imports, and publishing remain useful for collaboration. Task and research views show the same records; they are not a new catalogue to set up.

People can inspect the sources and work, narrow the audience or allowed actions, stop future work, or revoke a grant. Stopping cancels unsent effects and preserves confirmed receipts. Any reversal needs provider support and separate authority.

## 1. Get a useful answer before setting anything up

**Start.** A teammate opens an existing project page and asks: “What did we decide about launch readiness, and what still needs doing?” Slate already has permission to read the page and its linked rollout notes.

**What Slate does.** It finds relevant revisions, checks the accepted decision and latest checklist, then prepares an answer. The teammate need not import, structure, tag, or delegate the work.

**What the teammate sees.** “The security review is complete. The support handoff is still open, so I can’t confirm launch readiness yet. I also couldn’t confirm the latest support schedule.” Slate links the accepted launch criteria and checklist. “Why?” shows their revisions, owner, and what Slate checked.

If the schedule is material, Slate checks whether a supported calendar integration already exists, only the teammate’s reader permission is missing, or a new connection is needed. It reuses valid connections and grants and prepares the minimum useful connection and configuration itself. Only unavoidable provider sign-in, consent or credentials go to the named connection owner through the proper provider flow: “Connect the launch support schedule so I can confirm staffing. I don’t need the rest of the team calendar.” If only reader access is missing, it asks the responsible owner for that narrow permission. Slate never silently widens reader access. It validates the connected identity, scopes and teammate’s current access, fetches the schedule, and refreshes the answer without repeat approval for covered questions. People see ordinary language, never connector schemas or grant records.

**Done and pending.** The initial answer is useful before connection setup. If the owner declines, connection fails, or no supported connection exists, Slate preserves that bounded answer and names the actual missing schedule coverage. Slate owns the diagnostic and next step; it does not fabricate a source or hand the teammate a setup project. The support handoff stays with its owner, and staffing stays unconfirmed until the schedule supports it. Existing pages remain usable. Count unavoidable owner sign-in/consent or credential work and later administrator repair in total effort.

## 2. Answer an SSO inquiry, then complete only separately authorized work

**Start.** A specialist opens a customer SSO case in HappyToHelp and asks Slate: “Help me resolve Northstar’s SSO request and correct any outdated guidance.” The customer conversation and reply remain in HappyToHelp.

**What Slate does.** It checks the scoped pricing decision, release status, live account state, setup procedure, and article. It keeps a one-customer discount in scope and handles independently granted article maintenance separately. Publication requires its own exact grant and source audience rights; otherwise Slate leaves a patch tied to the corrected revision.

**What the specialist sees.** “Northstar’s Business plan is eligible for SSO at **[approved amount] per [billing interval]**. It is not enabled. Northstar’s IT owner needs to register its identity provider; then we can confirm sign-in against the account service. This was an inquiry: nothing was purchased or switched on. I corrected the outdated article and confirmed the live page now matches the approved terms.” Without a valid publication grant, Slate says: “I prepared the correction; the live article is unchanged.” HappyToHelp still owns the customer reply.

An account-authorized request to buy or activate SSO is a separate instruction. Slate checks the documented consent for that exact paid effect, verified purchase or entitlement, any required commercial approval, and a current grant for the exact operation on Northstar’s account. It acts only if all are present, then reads the account back. Otherwise it asks only the named owner for the missing decision or access and explains the consequence. A created request, customer-side setup, and confirmed enabled state remain distinct.

**Done and pending.** Inquiry and granted article repair can finish while activation is blocked. “Enabled” requires provider read-back and receipt; customer identity-provider setup can remain pending. Support text or a tool grant proves no price, consent, approval, or successful write.

## 3. Investigate a hard question, test a prepared experiment, and keep the learning

**Start.** A product leader asks: “Why is time to customer value rising, and what could reduce it without adding support work?”

**What Slate does.** It gathers permitted product, release, support, and customer evidence, then tests rival explanations in parallel. It finds that a release moved the “first value” event later in the workflow, explaining part of the apparent rise. It records that disconfirmation and checks for a remaining delay among comparable teams. The leader does not write hypotheses, create schemas, choose models, assign investigators, or design the experiment.

Slate reports early: “The headline increase is partly a measurement change. After correcting for it, teams with a handoff still appear slower; the evidence does not yet show why. So far there is no case for a blanket onboarding rewrite.” It prepares a clear choice: “Recommendation: offer one guided handoff to up to **[eligible number]** new teams over **[observation window]**, compared with the current process. I’ll keep outreach within **[contact limit]** and stop if support work passes **[guardrail]**. Estimated cost: **[reserved amount]**. This tests the handoff for these teams; it cannot prove a broad rollout will help, and the result may remain inconclusive.”

The owner can choose **Run this trial**, **Adjust**, or **Keep investigating**. If existing authority covers these exact bounds, Slate proceeds without asking again; otherwise the owner authorizes the effect. A broad research goal alone never permits customer contact or product changes. Slate records the effect and receipt, waits for the declared window, and checks the data.

**Later result.** “The trial did not show a reliable reduction in time to value. The sample was too small to rule out the improvement we said would matter, so this is inconclusive—not evidence that the handoff has no effect. No further outreach is scheduled. I updated the inquiry with the missing sample size and a better next measurement.” The prior forecast, counterexample, analysis, and revised method remain together so the next investigation can reproduce them.

**Done and pending.** Measurement correction and interim findings are complete; the trial waits for its window and checks. Benefit, precise null, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes stay distinct. A null counts against the proposed effect only when the test rules out the predeclared meaningful improvement; otherwise it remains inconclusive.

## 4. Make a policy decision together and let routine consequences settle quietly

**Start.** A policy owner and product lead open the existing pricing decision page. They discuss the customer impact in comments, compare the source evidence, and resolve the remaining question together: “Should the new SSO price take effect for renewals next month?”

**What Slate does.** Before acceptance, Slate prepares affected accounts, exceptions, dependent articles, procedures, skills, and open support work, then rehearses impact without live writes or publication. The people discuss and accept the decision in its shared page. The accepted revision stays immutable; a change supersedes it with a new decision.

After acceptance, Slate checks applicability, updates and tests routine internal summaries and procedures under standing grants, and records their revisions. It does not force every valid repair into a review queue. A valid, exact publication grant can authorize an external update; Slate checks source audience and container permission, publishes, and reads it back. Missing authority or a changed audience stays pending for its owner. Human **Verified** status needs exact-revision attestation.

**A quiet later day.** Today shows: “Three affected procedures were updated and checked. The customer exception was preserved. One public article is outside the existing publication grant and needs your decision.” Routine scans and successful subtasks do not fill Today; the owner can inspect impact and history without watching progress or rebuilding context.

**Done and pending.** Internal maintenance is complete with revision links and checks. The article is pending only because this audience is outside the existing grant; if the grant and source/container checks already cover it, Slate publishes and reads it back without a repeated approval. The summary never treats an unapproved option as policy or hides audience and pending state.

## 5. Help a new colleague finish real work and capture what the work teaches

**Start.** A customer-success colleague opens an assigned first-week onboarding task and asks: “What do I need to do next with this customer?” They do not attend a Slate setup or build a training database first.

**What Slate does.** It prepares a procedure from accepted decisions, customer state, and tested prior cases. The inconclusive trial in journey 3 remains evidence about a test, not an approved policy or onboarding rule. The colleague gets the next step, why it applies, its source revision, and who or what confirms completion.

**What the colleague sees.** “The welcome call is optional for this account. The current agreement is signed and the kickoff is booked for Thursday. Next, confirm the customer’s first shared task and record its owner. I’ll mark setup complete when the project space confirms access.” The colleague can open the accepted decision and exact procedure revision behind each step.

Slate guides the colleague through the kickoff and first shared task, checking choices against accepted terms and recording completion from the owning system. It does not mark work done because a procedure says it should. Missing human facts or actions stay with the responsible person. A confusing instruction becomes a focused, revision-linked repair; a valid grant lets Slate update and test it.

**What the team gains.** Work completion and useful exceptions are captured from the owning system; no retrospective note is required. The next colleague sees the corrected procedure with its decisions, rationale, and tests at the same source. No one writes a skill or duplicates an answer.

**Done and pending.** Only the colleague’s action or connected evidence completes a step. Customer configuration remains pending until verified. Machine checks never set Slate’s human **Verified** status; a person must open that revision and attest.

## 6. Resolve a contradiction or failed effect and route only the true decision

**Start.** A customer-success lead asks, “Did Northwind get the onboarding handoff, and what did we promise about a welcome call?” The accepted procedure says a call is optional, an old help page says every customer gets one, and the CRM’s current handoff status is unavailable.

**What Slate does.** It distinguishes the accepted procedure from the stale page, checks customer scope, and reads current CRM state. It repairs a granted internal derivative. It does not ask the lead whether the handoff happened when only CRM can establish that fact. If policy does not settle whether this customer class needs a call, Slate routes that choice to its named owner with the conflicting revisions, recommendation, and consequence.

Suppose Orbit’s authorized welcome-call task times out. Slate says: “The handoff update may have reached the CRM; I can’t confirm it yet. I’ve stopped further updates and am checking before trying again.” It reconciles the original receipt or an authorized read-back before resending. Without confirmation, it stays pending. Orbit retains CRM and outreach ownership; confirmed receipts remain. Reversal needs provider support and a separate valid grant.

**Done and pending.** Independent branches can finish around one failure. People decide policy; connected sources supply facts. Unusable work fails visibly; only transient transport failures receive AI-009’s bounded retries. After terminal failure, Slate offers a linked new run only when valid authority remains.
